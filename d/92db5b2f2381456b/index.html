Abstract

OBJECTIVE: To systematically evaluate the capability of three large language models (LLMs)—GPT-4o, Grok-3, and DeepSeek—in interpreting and translating clinical practice guidelines for caries management and in supporting clinical decision-making, thereby exploring their potential role in disseminating dental knowledge and assisting clinical practice. METHODS: Based on the American Dental Association’s guideline on nonrestorative treatments for carious lesions, a zero-shot prompting strategy—where models are given tasks without prior examples or fine-tuning—was employed to objectively assess their intrinsic ability to interpret and apply guideline knowledge. Each model was instructed to generate guideline summaries tailored for both healthcare professionals and the general public, as well as to provide diagnoses and treatment plans for three standardized clinical cases. All outputs were independently evaluated by three experienced endodontists across five dimensions (accuracy, clarity, conciseness, logical coherence, and overall quality) using a 0–10 scale. Automated text consistency was also assessed using ROUGE-L and BLEU metrics. RESULTS: In clinical case analyses, GPT-4o achieved a significantly higher composite score compared to DeepSeek and Grok-3, demonstrating superior diagnostic accuracy and clinically relevant treatment recommendations. This performance gap may be attributed to GPT-4o’s more robust clinical reasoning architecture and better alignment with guideline logic. For public-facing summaries, GPT-4o excelled in clarity and coherence, while DeepSeek achieved the highest accuracy in preserving source terminology. For professional summaries, all models performed comparably, with DeepSeek leading in automated metrics. CONCLUSION: Large language models demonstrate promising potential in translating dental guidelines and assisting clinical decision-making, with GPT-4o showing particularly strong performance in complex clinical reasoning tasks. However, limitations include variability in personalized recommendation generation and occasional mechanistic application of guidelines. The findings underscore the need for enhanced model interpretability, integration of multimodal data, and development of human-AI collaborative frameworks to optimize the balance between standardized and personalized oral disease management.

Introduction

Dental caries is a highly prevalent global disease and a primary initiating factor for pulp and periapical pathologies. The standardized management of caries is therefore crucial for preventing the progression to more complex endodontic conditions [1–3]. Pulp and periapical diseases themselves are common occurrences in dentistry. The accuracy of their diagnosis is closely associated with treatment efficacy, directly impacting patients’ masticatory function, oral health, and quality of life [4–6]. For a long time, the diagnosis of these conditions has been heavily reliant on clinicians’ experience and the subjective interpretation of radiographic findings, leading to variability among practitioners of different experience levels and affecting the consistency and accuracy of diagnosis and treatment [7–12]. The treatment process also faces challenges such as the complex anatomy of the root canal system and the limitations of equipment precision. Furthermore, postoperative prognosis assessment is often based on generalized experience, lacking personalized predictive tools. In recent years, the rapid advancement of digital technology has brought about a revolutionary shift in the diagnosis of endodontic diseases [13–15]. Three-dimensional imaging technologies, such as intraoral scanning, have significantly improved the visualization of tooth structure. Meanwhile, artificial intelligence, particularly the widespread application of deep learning models in medical image analysis, has provided novel approaches for the automated detection of caries and periapical lesions and for assisted decision-making, substantially enhancing diagnostic efficiency and standardization [16]. Within this context, Large Language Models (LLMs), as a pivotal branch of natural language processing, have demonstrated the potential to integrate and translate specialized medical knowledge, promising to further advance the precision and accessibility of oral healthcare [17]. However, while prior studies have primarily tested LLMs on dental examinations or knowledge recall, there remains a lack of rigorous assessment of their ability to interpret and apply complex clinical guidelines, perform stepwise clinical reasoning, and generate personalized treatment plans in realistic clinical scenarios [18]. This represents a critical research gap, as the true potential of LLMs in clinical decision support lies not merely in factual retrieval, but in their capacity to synthesize guidelines, reason through patient-specific presentations, and provide actionable, tailored recommendations. Therefore, to address this gap, this study aims to systematically evaluate three major AI platforms—GPT-4o (GPT-4o), Grok-3, and DeepSeek—based on the American Dental Association’s Evidence-Based Clinical Practice Guideline on Nonrestorative Treatments for Carious Lesions and the Australian Society of Endodontology’s Guidelines for Non-Surgical Root Canal Treatment. It assesses their capabilities in interpreting professional guidelines and generating summaries for different target audiences, as well as their reasoning and decision-support abilities in clinical case management, aiming to explore the application prospects of LLMs in enhancing knowledge dissemination and clinical practice in dentistry [19, 20].

Methods › Study design and guideline selection

This study employed a comparative analytical design to evaluate the capabilities of three advanced LLMs—GPT-4o, Grok-3, and DeepSeek—in interpreting and translating complex clinical practice guidelines into actionable summaries for different target audiences, as well as generating diagnoses and treatment plans for standardized clinical cases. The source documents for this evaluation were the Evidence-Based Clinical Practice Guideline on Nonrestorative Treatments for Carious Lesions (2018) published by the American Dental Association (ADA) and the Guidelines for Non-Surgical Root Canal Treatment (2024) issued by the Australian Society of Endodontology Inc. These specific guidelines were selected based on their high level of international recognition, their rigorous evidence-based methodology, and their publication dates, ensuring they represent the most current and authoritative standards in the field. The ADA (2018) guideline is a seminal work in carious lesion management, while the Australian (2024) guideline offers the most recent evidence for endodontic treatment. Their main content, including key recommendations for diagnosis and treatment planning, was systematically extracted and is summarized in supplement to provide context for the evaluation.

Methods › Large language model selection and interaction

The three LLMs—GPT-4o, Grok-3, and DeepSeek—were selected to represent a cross-section of currently leading and accessible large language models. GPT-4o was chosen for its established state-of-the-art performance in complex reasoning and language generation. Grok-3 was selected for its unique real-time knowledge integration capabilities. DeepSeek was included to represent a competitive, high-performance model from a different development ecosystem, providing a broader comparative perspective. All models were accessed via their public application programming interfaces (APIs) between June 1 and June 10, 2025. The temperature parameter was set to 0 for all models to ensure reproducibility and minimize output randomness. Token usage was tracked per query but was not a constraining factor for this analysis. All interactions were conducted in Chinese to ensure consistency and practical applicability within the primary research context. A standardized zero-shot prompting strategy was applied across all tasks to objectively evaluate the models’ intrinsic capabilities without extensive task-specific fine-tuning. Each model was prompted to generate two distinct summaries of the guidelines: one tailored for oral healthcare professionals (e.g., junior dentists, dental students), requiring technical accuracy and inclusion of clinical details; and another aimed at the general public. (STable 1 and STable 2).

Following the summary task, each model was provided with three standardized clinical vignettes depicting patients with various complexities of carious lesions and symptoms of pulpitis. These were simulated cases and did not involve real patient data, thereby obviating the need for ethical approval or informed consent. The outputs of each model were evaluated across five dimensions—accuracy, clarity, conciseness, logical coherence, and overall quality—using a manual scoring system ranging from 0 to 10 points. (STable 3 and STable 4).

The ability to convey necessary information without unnecessary or redundant text (0 = overly verbose/irrelevant, 10 = maximally concise).

The logical flow of ideas, from diagnosis to treatment plan, and the internal consistency of the argument (0 = completely illogical, 10 = perfectly logical and coherent).

Methods › Evaluation metrics and scoring system › Manual evaluation by expert assessors

A holistic judgment of the output’s usefulness and professionalism as a clinical decision support tool (0 = unacceptable, 10 = exemplary). The manual evaluations were conducted independently by three assessors, all of whom are licensed dentists with over three years of clinical experience specializing in operative dentistry and endodontics. The assessors were blinded to the LLM that generated each output to prevent bias. The final score for each dimension for a given output was calculated as the average of the three assessors’ scores.

Methods › Evaluation metrics and scoring system › Automated evaluation metrics

Additionally, automated metrics including ROUGE-L and BLEU were employed to assess surface-level textual similarity between the model-generated professional summaries and a reference summary written by an expert endodontist. We acknowledge that ROUGE and BLEU are primarily metrics for n-gram overlap and are limited in their ability to capture semantic depth, clinical reasoning, or creative adaptation for lay audiences. Their use in this study is justified as a supplementary, objective measure of lexical and phrasal alignment with an expert-generated gold standard, providing a baseline for textual consistency, but they are not considered a primary measure of quality, which is better assessed by the expert manual evaluation.

Methods › Statistical analysis

To determine the statistical significance of performance differences between the three LLMs, the average scores from the manual evaluations were analyzed. A one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) test was conducted for each of the five evaluation dimensions (accuracy, clarity, conciseness, logical coherence, overall quality) across the three models. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software.

Results › Analysis of medical documentation (for non-medical audiences)

In the task of translating caries management guidelines for non-medical audiences, GPT-4o demonstrated superior performance, achieving an overall score of 9.03 (Table 1). It excelled particularly in clarity and logical coherence. Its content was well-structured and employed effective terminology simplification strategies, such as comparing early caries lesions to “white spot lesions,” which significantly improved public comprehension. DeepSeek scored 8.83 in accuracy and 9.0 in logical coherence (Table 1). Its outputs showed high fidelity to the American Dental Association guidelines, accurately reflecting professional details such as fluoride concentration, the ICDAS classification system, and indications for silver diamine fluoride. However, its use of excessive professional terminology (e.g., remineralization and resin infiltration techniques) posed cognitive barriers for non-expert readers. Although Grok-3 offers advantages in open access and cost-effectiveness, it received a composite score of only 8.37. It scored merely 8.0 and 8.17 in conciseness and clarity, respectively, exhibiting significant issues with content redundancy—such as repeatedly emphasizing sugar reduction—and failed to clearly distinguish treatment priorities between primary and permanent teeth. Automated metric validation revealed that DeepSeek achieved the highest ROUGE-L and BLEU scores (0.89 and 0.85, respectively), indicating the strongest consistency with the original guideline (Table 2; Figs. 1e and f and 2d, e and f). While GPT-4o performed excellently in linguistic adaptability, it occasionally lacked detail in presenting specific treatment frequencies and sources of evidence. Figures 1a-c and 2a-c visually compare the models’ performance profiles across these key dimensions. Overall, current LLMs show significant potential in translating medical guidelines for public use but require further optimization in balancing terminology control, detail completeness, and audience appropriateness. Results › Analysis of medical documentation (for junior doctors and medical students)

In the evaluation of caries guideline translation capabilities for junior dentists and medical students, the outputs of three LLMs—GPT-4o, DeepSeek, and Grok-3—were systematically analyzed across five dimensions: accuracy, clarity, conciseness, logical coherence, and overall quality. The results indicated that all three models achieved high accuracy scores of 9.5 (Table 3), demonstrating strong adherence to the original guideline’s professional recommendations and evidence grades, particularly in rigorously presenting non-restorative treatment recommendations. In terms of clarity, GPT-4o led with a perfect score, offering clear structure and standardized terminology that significantly enhanced comprehension efficiency for medical trainees; both DeepSeek and Grok-3 also performed excellently, scoring 9.5. For conciseness, DeepSeek and Grok-3 scored 9.0, slightly higher than GPT-4o’s 8.5 (Table 3), indicating better information condensation capabilities. In logical coherence, GPT-4o again ranked first with a perfect score by strictly adhering to the clinical reasoning pathway of “etiology – clinical presentation – diagnosis – treatment – prognosis.” DeepSeek followed closely with a score of 9.75, demonstrating excellent reasoning continuity (Table 3). All three models received an overall quality score of 9.5, indicating general reliability in medical knowledge translation. Automated metrics showed that DeepSeek achieved slightly higher ROUGE-L and BLEU scores (0.85 and 0.81, respectively) than GPT-4o and Grok-3 (Table 4), reflecting better semantic and linguistic alignment with the guideline text. In summary, although GPT-4o held a slight advantage in clarity and logical coherence, the two open-source models demonstrated significant potential in comprehensive performance and accessibility, making them particularly suitable for generating professional content in medical education that balances accuracy, comprehensibility, and efficiency. Results › Analysis of diagnosis and treatment in clinical cases

In the analysis of diagnosis and treatment planning for actual clinical cases, systematic comparison of the three large language models across multiple caries-related cases revealed significant differences in their ability to translate caries management guidelines into clinical practice. Overall, GPT-4o performed optimally in standardized processing protocols, diagnostic accuracy, and rationality of treatment plans, achieving a composite score of 92.51, significantly higher than Grok-3’s 68.67 and DeepSeek’s 50.67 (Table 6; Fig. 3e and f). Specifically, GPT-4o effectively identified key clinical manifestations and radiographic features in case analyses and provided stratified treatment recommendations based on guidelines. Particularly in complex cases—such as deep caries with pulp exposure or periapical pathology—its proposed root canal treatment and subsequent restorative plans adhered to clinical standards. Although Grok-3 demonstrated some clinical logic in certain cases (e.g., understanding the application of non-restorative treatments like fluoride), its diagnostic details and plan completeness were weaker. It failed to adequately integrate dynamic monitoring and intervention strategies from the guidelines, especially in cases complicated by pulpal pathology. DeepSeek exhibited strong capabilities in using technical terminology, accurately referencing professional concepts such as ICDAS classification and fluoride agent concentrations. However, its recommendations often lacked clinical applicability and demonstrated a mechanistic application of guidelines. This was particularly evident in its failure to correctly distinguish the applicability boundaries between “non-restorative treatments for caries” and “treatments for periapical diseases,” leading to knowledge transfer errors. For instance, it mechanically suggested non-restorative treatments for periapical cases without obvious carious lesions, reflecting a limited ability to tailor guideline recommendations to specific case characteristics. The variation in performance across different clinical cases is illustrated in Fig. 3c and d. Furthermore, in completeness of instructions, GPT-4o also led with a score of 9.17 (Table 6). Its postoperative guidance covered pain management, oral hygiene maintenance, and long-term follow-up requirements. In contrast, Grok-3 and DeepSeek scored only 7.33 and 6.17, respectively, showing significant deficiencies in personalized preventive measures and key patient communication points. The comparative performance across the four core clinical dimensions (standardization, diagnosis, treatment selection, and order completeness) is visually summarized in Fig. 3a and b. To statistically validate the performance differences among the three LLMs, a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted on the total clinical case scores (maximum 100) across the three models. The results revealed a highly statistically significant overall difference among the platforms (F = 90.86, p < 0.001). Descriptive statistics indicated that GPT-4o achieved the highest mean score (92.5 ± 1.98), followed by Grok-3 (68.7 ± 5.01), while DeepSeek obtained the lowest mean score (50.7 ± 7.63). Post-hoc comparisons (LSD or Tukey HSD) demonstrated that the score differences between any two platforms were statistically significant (p < 0.05 for all pairwise comparisons). These findings indicate that in simulated clinical diagnostic and treatment tasks, GPT-4o’s comprehensive performance significantly surpasses that of Grok-3 and DeepSeek, whereas DeepSeek exhibited relative deficiencies in standardization, accuracy, and completeness. Specifically examining the sub-dimensions (treatment protocol standardization, diagnostic accuracy, treatment selection, and completeness of medical instructions), DeepSeek consistently scored lower across most clinical cases, particularly falling below Grok-3 and GPT-4o in the first three metrics. In conclusion, under the current testing conditions, GPT-4o most closely approximates the professional standard (total score 100), while DeepSeek still has considerable room for improvement. In conclusion, current LLMs still face multiple challenges in clinical reasoning for caries and endodontics, including accurate interpretation of guideline recommendations, generation of individualized treatment strategies, and supporting shared decision-making with patients. Their practical application requires integration with professional judgment to ensure diagnostic and therapeutic quality and patient safety. Conclusions and future perspectives

This systematic evaluation of three large language models in translating caries and endodontic management guidelines and supporting clinical decision-making provides several important insights into the current capabilities and limitations of LLMs in oral healthcare. Our findings reveal a nuanced landscape where model performance varies significantly across task types and clinical contexts, reflecting fundamental differences in their underlying architectures and training approaches. The superior performance of GPT-4o in clinical case analysis (92.51/100), particularly its more accurate diagnostic reasoning and treatment recommendations, likely stems from its advanced reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) architecture and more extensive training on diverse clinical corpora. This aligns with recent findings by Kayaalp et al. [21], who demonstrated that GPT-4o with retrieval-augmented generation achieved 92.1% accuracy on cardiology guideline-based questions, significantly outperforming models without such capabilities. In contrast, DeepSeek’s tendency to mechanically apply non-restorative treatment recommendations in cases of asymptomatic periapical lesions reveals a critical limitation: the model appears to prioritize surface-level guideline matching over deeper pathophysiological reasoning. This error pattern suggests that DeepSeek, despite its strong performance in structured knowledge recall, struggles with the nuanced task of determining guideline applicability boundaries—a cognitive process that in human clinicians involves integrating multiple knowledge domains including pathology, anatomy, and patient-specific factors. The observed confusion in DeepSeek between “non-restorative treatment” and “apical periodontal treatment” exemplifies a reasoning failure that can be understood through the lens of dual-process theory in clinical cognition [22]. Human clinicians typically employ System 1 (intuitive, pattern-based) reasoning for straightforward cases but engage System 2 (analytical, deliberative) reasoning when navigating complex presentations or guideline boundaries. Our error analysis suggests that DeepSeek’s failure stems from an inability to appropriately transition between these reasoning modes—it incorrectly applies guideline recommendations derived from typical presentations (System 1 pattern matching) without engaging in the necessary analytical verification of whether the clinical scenario falls within the guideline’s intended scope. This interpretation is supported by recent work from Amann et al. [23], who identified similar reasoning failures in DeepSeek R1, including anchoring bias and difficulty integrating conflicting data across 100 clinical cases. Comparing our findings with existing healthcare LLM research reveals both consistencies and important domain-specific insights. The performance hierarchy we observed (GPT-4o > DeepSeek > Grok-3) mirrors results from a recent multicenter evaluation of LLMs in transfusion medicine, where GPT-4o demonstrated significantly higher accuracy (0.6) compared to other models, though still below human expert performance (0.8) [24]. Similarly, Naliyatthaliyazchayil et al. [25] found that among non-reasoning models, GPT-4 achieved 84.7% accuracy in primary diagnosis generation, while DeepSeek-R1 (a reasoning-optimized variant) showed comparable performance in readmission risk prediction. However, our study extends these findings by specifically examining guideline interpretation and application—a more complex task than factual recall or diagnosis alone—and by identifying domain-specific error patterns unique to dental clinical reasoning. These findings carry important implications for the integration of LLMs into clinical decision support. The variability we observed across models and tasks underscores that current LLMs cannot be viewed as interchangeable tools; their suitability for clinical applications depends critically on the specific cognitive demands of the task. More importantly, the error patterns we identified—particularly DeepSeek’s mechanical guideline application—highlight a potential safety risk: LLMs may amplify guideline misuse when deployed without appropriate safeguards. As Roeschl et al. [26] demonstrated in their study of LLMs for aortic stenosis treatment decisions, even advanced models showed poor agreement with heart team decisions when presented with real-world clinical data, with hallucinations observed across all tested models. This reinforces the irreplaceable role of human clinical judgment in AI-assisted decision support and suggests that LLMs should function as decision aids that highlight relevant guidelines while deferring final integration of patient-specific factors to clinicians. Several important limitations must be acknowledged. First, while we employed three experienced endodontists for manual evaluation and reported average scores, scorer subjectivity remains a potential source of bias. Although we attempted to mitigate this through standardized scoring criteria, future studies should incorporate formal inter-rater reliability metrics such as intraclass correlation coefficients. Second, the limited number of clinical cases (three) constrains the generalizability of our findings; as noted by Naliyatthaliyazchayil et al. [25], robust evaluation of LLM clinical performance requires larger sample sizes to detect statistically meaningful differences. Third, our use of zero-shot prompting alone, without exploring alternative strategies such as few-shot learning or chain-of-thought prompting, may not capture the full potential of these models. Recent evidence suggests that chain-of-thought prompting can significantly enhance LLM reasoning in clinical contexts by making the cognitive process explicit and amenable to verification [27]. Future work should systematically compare zero-shot, few-shot, and chain-of-thought prompting strategies in simulated clinical settings to identify optimal interaction paradigms for guideline-based decision support. Fourth, the absence of real-world clinical validation means our findings, while informative, may not directly translate to actual clinical settings where patient complexity, time constraints, and information uncertainty create additional challenges. Finally, our evaluation focused exclusively on text-based interactions, whereas real-world clinical decision support increasingly involves multimodal data integration (imaging, molecular diagnostics, patient-reported outcomes). In addition to the above limitations, the current study did not explore the potential synergistic effects of combining different prompting strategies with model fine-tuning or retrieval-augmented generation. Given that clinical decision support often requires handling of out-of-distribution cases and dynamic updating of knowledge, future research should also investigate adaptive prompting frameworks that can adjust their strategies based on case complexity and the model’s confidence estimates. Future development should focus on constructing multimodal data integration and dynamic knowledge-updating mechanisms. Firstly, efforts should promote the synergistic analysis of LLMs with imaging, molecular diagnostics, and real-time monitoring data. For example, incorporating cone-beam CT (CBCT) imaging features and biomarkers of caries activity into decision-making systems could enhance the accuracy of pulp status interpretation. Current studies have already shown high consistency (exceeding 90% accuracy) between AI and clinicians in detecting and segmenting chronic periapical lesions [28]. Secondly, it is essential to establish automated pipelines linking guideline updates to model retraining, ensuring treatment recommendations remain synchronized with advances in evidence-based medicine. Simultaneously, greater emphasis must be placed on the complementary roles of clinical experience and AI-driven decision support, fostering the development of human-AI collaborative care models with clinicians at the helm. This will enhance the models’ capacity to predict individual variations in treatment outcomes. Furthermore, challenges related to technological generalizability and equity must be addressed to ensure that AI-driven improvements in diagnosis and treatment are accessible even in resource-limited settings. The ultimate goal is to develop intelligent clinical systems that embody precision, adaptability, and ethical compliance, thereby unifying standardized and personalized approaches to the prevention and management of diseases such as dental caries and periapical pathologies.
