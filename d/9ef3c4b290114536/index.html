Abstract

AbstractThis cross-sectional study investigated characteristics and reporting of updates to artificial intelligence (AI)/machine learning–based medical devices cleared through the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) 501(k) pathway.

Introduction

The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has cleared more than 1000 artificial intelligence and/or machine learning (AI/ML)–based medical devices, mainly through the 510(k) pathway.1 AI/ML-based devices may undergo algorithmic updates that alter their performance or intended use over time. Most studies have examined the initial FDA clearance of AI/ML-based medical devices, but less is known about how and when these devices are updated after clearance.2,3 We investigated the nature, frequency, timing, and reporting of updates to AI/ML-based medical devices cleared through the 510(k) pathway.

Methods

This cross-sectional study used publicly available data from the FDA database; therefore, review and approval by an institutional review board or ethics committee were not required under French law. The study is reported following the STROBE reporting guideline. We analyzed a previously identified cohort of medical devices explicitly using AI/ML that were cleared through the 510(k) pathway between January 9, 2019, and December 29, 2021.4,5 One reviewer (M.Z.) searched the 510(k) database and extracted all entries, including initial clearances and modification requests, corresponding to these devices up to March 31, 2025. For each modification request, 2 reviewers (M.Z. and V.T.T.) assessed whether it involved an update of the AI/ML algorithm and, if so, extracted relevant data. We categorized the nature of updates as those that changed the device’s previous usage vs those that involved only improvement of algorithm performance. We then described the frequency and timing of updates. Finally, we assessed the reporting of these updates in FDA documents (eMethods in Supplement 1). All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.5.3 (R Project for Statistical Computing).

Results

We included 163 unique devices that explicitly reported using AI/ML with initial clearance between May 12, 2015, and December 29, 2021, and retrieved 352 related entries in the 510(k) database. Most devices (89.0%) were intended to analyze medical imaging data, for example to detect anomalies. Among these devices, 56 (34.4%) received at least 1 update to their AI/ML algorithm, corresponding to 106 entries in the 510(k) database (range, 1-13 updates per device), with a median (IQR) time until first update of 1.8 (0.9-2.5) years (Figure). Of the 56 devices that received at least 1 update, 42 (75.0%) received at least 1 update that changed their usage compared with the previous version. Among these 95 usage updates (from 81 entries given that some entries could report multiple usage changes), 23 (24.2%) indicated the addition of new features (eg, introducing an AI-powered alert system in a medical imaging device for image classification) and 72 (75.8%) indicated a change in the algorithm input (eg, adding blood pressure data to an algorithm that analyzed electrocardiogram waveforms). A total of 9 devices (16.1%) had updates that involved only improvements in algorithm performance. The 21 entries that described such improvement comprised 10 (47.6%) indicating the addition of new data, 8 (38.1%) indicating the addition of new neural networks, and 3 (14.3%) indicating changes in the AI/ML algorithm architecture (eg, switching from a convolutional network to a transformer-based model). Additionally, for 5 devices (8.9%) corresponding to 12 entries, there was no clear description of how the AI/ML algorithm was updated (Table). Regarding the reporting of performance assessment of updates of AI/ML devices in FDA databases (among the 106 entries and 56 devices with an update), information on participants whose data were used to assess performance were absent from 41 entries (38.7%), so 24 devices (42.9%) had no information on that point in any entry related to their updates. Sample size was not reported in 38 entries (35.8%), so 15 devices (26.8%) had no information on that point in any entry related to their updates. Metrics and results of this performance evaluation were not reported in 14 entries (13.2%), so 8 devices (14.3%) had no information on that point in any entry related to their updates. Finally, organizational or clinical benefits of the update were not reported in 86 entries (81.1%), so 48 devices (85.7%) provided no information on that point in any entry related to their updates.

Discussion

In this cross-sectional study, one-third of included AI/ML devices received at least 1 update to their algorithm, with a median time until first update of 1.8 years. To our knowledge, this is the first mapping of updates of AI/ML devices requiring a modification request to the FDA. We showed that most updated devices underwent at least 1 change in their use. Of note, our study did not appraise all updates of AI/ML devices given that the FDA Predetermined Change Control Plan guidance permits developers to prespecify modifications, allowing a limited set of changes without new submission.6 Our study also revealed heterogeneity in the reporting of performance assessments for updates to AI/ML-based devices, which may limit physician and patient ability to assess and choose these devices. Limitations included the use of a previously identified cohort1 of devices, small sample size, and potential misclassification because our assessment relied solely on information reported in FDA documents. Furthermore, we could not determine whether devices without identified updates had undergone modifications that were not associated with a corresponding FDA modification request. Although updates to AI/ML-based medical devices were common, substantial heterogeneity remains in FDA documents regarding the reporting of modifications and their association with device performance. Strengthening and standardizing reporting requirements, together with establishing and enforcing postdeployment monitoring, are critical steps in ensuring the safety and effectiveness of AI/ML-based medical devices over time.
