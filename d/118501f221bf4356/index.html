Abstract

Clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) is an aggressive malignant tumor. Disulfidptosis is a new programmed cell death mechanism, which is characterized by the abnormal accumulation of intracellular disulfides that are highly toxic to cells. However, the contribution of disulfidptosis to ccRCC progression has not been fully clarified. In this study, two different molecular subtypes related to disulfidptosis were identified in ccRCC patients by the non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) algorithm. The cluster 1 was characterized by a worse prognosis and higher mRNAsi levels. Then, difference analysis and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) were conducted to search modular genes that are highly associated with tumor stemness and tumor microenvironment. Subsequently, a SADG signature containing nine genes was constructed stepwise by WGCNA and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) Cox regression analysis. The high-risk score group had a worse outcome, and immune regulation and metabolic signatures might be responsible for cancer progression in the high-risk group. After that, a predictive nomogram was constructed, and the predicting power of the risk model was verified using inter and three independent external validation datasets. Nine SADGs were shown to significantly correlate with immune infiltration, tumor mutation burden (TMB), microsatellite instability (MSI) and immune checkpoint. In addition, based on the single-cell RNA sequencing dataset (GSE139555), the distribution and expression of nine hub genes in various types of immune cells were analyzed. Finally, the expression level of the nine genes was verified in clinical samples by qRT-PCR.

Introduction

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is a malignant solid tumor with a high prevalence. In 2023, it is estimated that more than 70,000 new cases of RCC will be diagnosed, along with 13,780 deaths, according to the survey of US statistics [1]. RCC consists of three important histological subtypes—clear cell RCC (ccRCC), papillary RCC, and chromophobe RCC. Among these, ccRCC accounts for more than 70% of RCC incidence [2,3]. Surgery remains the primary therapy for early-stage localized ccRCC patients, yet more than 30% of them suffer from post-surgery recurrence or develop metastases [4,5]. Radiotherapy and chemotherapy are not effective enough in treating advanced metastatic renal cancer, so the clinical management for those patients is extremely limited with a 5-year survival rate of 13% [6]. Recently, targeted therapy and immunotherapy have brought encouraging hope for advanced-stage ccRCC. Current treatments, however, lack specificity, and drug resistance often occurs. Traditional targeted treatment and immunotherapy remain to be ineffective for some patients, leading to generally poor prognoses [7,8]. Thus, exploring the mechanism and prognostic markers of ccRCC is a promising direction for further kidney cancer research. Tumorigenesis and progression are regulated by the active and controlled cell death process known as programmed cell death (PCD), which is driven by genes [9]. Disulfidptosis, a novel mechanism of cell death, is mediated by the susceptibility of the actin cytoskeleton to disulfide stress, which occurs with the unusual accumulation of disulfides and can be harmful to cells, inhibiting the survival and proliferation of cancer cells [10]. In glucose-starved cells with overexpressed SLC7A11, disulfidptosis is triggered by the accumulation of intracellular disulfides. Liu et al. found that increased SLC7A11 expression in renal cancer cells under glucose deprivation promotes the depletion of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) in the cytoplasm [11]. NADPH often offers reducing power to combat disulfide stress and keep cells in a state of equilibrium. However, in the absence of glucose, the pentose phosphate pathway (PPP)'s ability to produce NADPH from glucose is constrained. High rates of cystine uptake mediated by overexpressed SLC7A11 further depletes intracellular NADPH, causing its depletion, which induces multiple intermolecular disulfide-bond formation between actin cytoskeleton proteins and filament (F-actin) contraction, inducing disulfide stress and triggering disulfidptosis, which is toxic to cells [12]. These studies illustrated the significance of disulfidptosis to tumor progression, which may be a focus of cancer immunotherapy in the future. Besides, one of the most important characteristics influencing cancer metastasis is cancer stemness. A particularly promising strategy for cancer treatment is to target cancer stemness [13]. Meanwhile, the development and proliferation of malignant tumors are related to features of the tumor microenvironment, and systemic therapy responses may be impacted by these characteristics [14]. As a new cell death, disulfidptosis holds importance in exploring potentially powerful therapeutic strategies for cancer. The relationship between disulfidptosis and ccRCC patients remains unknown. In our study, we sought to thoroughly explore the molecular characteristics of disulfidptosis-related genes (DRGs) in renal cancer. This study demonstrates the significance of DRGs in the progression of renal cancer and establishes a basis for the therapeutic application of disulfidptosis regulators in kidney cancer. We established and validated a stemness-associated disulfidptosis gene (SADG) signature, which demonstrated high accuracy in estimating the long-term survival of ccRCC patients and response to immunotherapy with independent cohorts. This study holds great potential to provide a unique understanding of the significance of SADG in ccRCC progression and paves the way for the therapeutic application of disulfidptosis regulators in renal cancer.

Materials and methods

External cohorts used for model validation.

Materials and methods › Data download and processing

The RNA-seq and clinical data of TCGA-KIRC patients were downloaded from the cancer genome atlas (TCGA) website (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). External validation datasets were downloaded from IMVIGOR210 (http://research-pub.gene.com/), ICGC-RECA (https://dcc.icgc.org/) and E-MTAB-1980 (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/). The characteristics of eligible ccRCC patients included in this study were summarized in Supplementary Table 1. Single-cell sequencing data of ccRCC comes from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/, GSE139555). Before analysis, all TCGA RNA-seq data was normalized into transcripts per million (TPM) data. The patients with incomplete clinical survival information and expression were excluded from our study. A total of 88 disulfidptosis-related genes were obtained based on published literature (Supplementary Table 2) [11]. Genetic mutation data, including single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) and copy number variation (CNV), was downloaded from the UCSC Xena database (https://xena.ucsc.edu/).

Materials and methods › NMF clustering and survival analysis

Using the R package “coxph” to conduct univariate Cox analysis to find prognosis-related genes (p < 0.05). The non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) clustering was conducted based on expression data of 44 disulfidptosis-related genes (DRGs) to clustering TCGA-RCC samples (rank = 2:10, method = “brunet, nrun = 10”). After considering indicators such as cophenetic, dispersion, and silhouette, the best K value (K = 2) was determined. Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival analysis was conducted to identify the Overall survival (OS) difference between different NMF clusters.

Materials and methods › Prognostic risk model construction

All differential expression analysis was performed using the R package “DEseq2” with the following thresholds: adjusted p-value <0.01, |log2 fold change |> 1. The study initially conducted a difference analysis between two groups, NMF-cluster C1 and C2. Subsequently, the researchers employed weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) to identify the co-expression module correlated with tumor stemness and tumor microenvironment [15]. Candidate module genes were identified by intersecting the key module genes obtained through WGCNA analysis with the differential genes of cancer and para-cancer in ccRCC patients. Subsequently, genes associated with ccRCC patients' overall survival were screened using univariate analysis. Finally, a stemness-associated disulfidptosis gene (SADG) signature for ccRCC patients was built using the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Operator (LASSO) Cox regression analysis. The risk score is evaluated according to the following formula: Riskscore = ∑i=xnCoe(genei)*Exp(genei). Coe(genei): the coefficient of each gene in the SADG signature. Exp (genei): the relative expression level of each gene in this study. After that, ccRCC patients were divided into high- and low-risk groups based on the optimal cut-off value for risk score. Kaplan-Meier analysis was conducted to compare the survival rate between high- and low-risk score groups. A nomogram was developed after LASSO and multivariate Cox regression analysis, which integrated the SADG signature and clinicopathological characteristics to predict survival probability. Next, using the R package “ggDCA” to draw decision curve analysis (DCA) and evaluate the predicting power and clinical benefit of the nomogram.

Materials and methods › Internal and external validation of the prognostic model

Patients with ccRCC from the TCGA were randomly allocated to training and test cohorts at a 7:3 ratio. In our study, in order to compare the overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in low- and high-risk cohorts, we conducted the survival analysis using the R packages "survminer" and "survival". Furthermore, clinical information correlated with the prognosis of patients was combined with riskscore to draw forest charts. Additionally, external datasets were used to validate the SADG signature. Materials and methods › Functional enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes

The R package “ClusterProfiler” was utilized to conduct GSEA analysis between high- and low-risk score groups. The annotation files were downloaded from MSigDB (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb) and ConsensusPathDB (http://cpdb.molgen.mpg.de/) database.

Materials and methods › Immune infiltration analysis

In this study, the R package “ESTIMATE” was utilized for the estimation of tumor purity and stromal components in the tumor microenvironment (TME) between NMF-cluster C1 and C2 [16]. Moreover, we identified the different infiltrated immune cells between the high- and low-risk score groups based on the CIBERSORT algorithm (https://cibersort.stanford.edu/).

Materials and methods › Exploring of hub genes by single-cell sequencing

The Tumor Immune Single-Cell Hub (TISCH) (http://tisch.comp-genomics.org) is a scRNA-seq database that attempts to characterize TME at single-cell resolution. With corresponding clinical data, TISCH has more than 70 high-quality single-cell transcriptome datasets of 27 cancers. Based on this database, we analyzed the GSE139555 dataset to explore the heterogeneous expression pattern of nine prognostic genes in ccRCC cells and different immune cells at the single-cell level [17].

Materials and methods › Validation of nine SADGs by GEPIA

The Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) database (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/index.html) was utilized for further validation and survival analysis. Based on the TCGA and GTEx databases, the information of gene expression for ccRCC and normal samples was gathered. Survival analysis was conducted based on the survival data of the TCGA database.

Materials and methods › Collection of human tissue specimens and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Authorized by the ethics committee of the affiliated Yantai Yuhuangding Hospital of Qingdao University (No.2023-314), ccRCC tissues and adjacent normal samples from eight patients who underwent radical or partial nephrectomy were gathered. Two different pathologists independently verified the pathological specimens. Using SteadyPure Quick RNA Extraction Kit (Accurate Biology, China), we extracted total RNA from clinical samples. Subsequently, The SYBR Green Premix Pro Taq HS qPCR Kit and Rox Reference Dye (Accurate Biology, China) were used for the qRT-PCR procedure. Based on the 2®−ΔΔCT calculation method, we calculated the mRNA expression of core genes. For each experiment, three multiple holes were set. The primer sequences used can be found in Supplementary Table 3.

Materials and methods › Statistics

All of the bioinformatics analyses were conducted using the software R version 4.2.0. For comparisons between two independent samples and comparisons among multiple samples for non-parametric data, respectively, we utilized the Wilcoxon test and the Kruskal–Wallis test. For parametric data, t-tests and one-way ANOVA were utilized. We conducted the survival study using the Kaplan-Meier method and the log-rank test. For each analysis, p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results › Identification and vilification of SADG signature

Firstly, differentially expressed genes (DEGs) analysis was conducted between NMF-cluster 1 and NMF-cluster 2, and 11509 DEGs were obtained (Fig. 3A, Supplementary Table 4). WGCNA is a systems biology method used to describe gene association patterns between different samples, which can be used to identify gene sets with highly synergistic changes and to identify candidate organisms based on the interconnection of gene sets and the relationship between gene sets and phenotypes marker genes or therapeutic targets. In our study, we used WCGNA to screen the DEGs related to cell stemness and TME by obtaining the most relevant modules (Fig. 3B). As shown in Fig. 3C, the co-expression pink module was significantly positively associated with cell stemness and TME (Supplementary Table 5). Next, differentially expressed analysis was carried out in cancer and para-cancer tissues in TCGA-KIRC transcriptome data (Fig. 3D). By taking the intersection of the up-expressed genes in tumor tissues and hubgenes from WGCNA, we obtained 71 stemness and TME associated disulfidptosis-related DEGs. After that, we screened genes that were closely correlated with OS based on univariate Cox regression analysis (Fig. 3E and F). Finally, LASSO and multiCox analysis identified nine signature genes: MYO1F, FMNL1, FCGR1B, TNFAIP8L2, CYTH4, NCF4, TNFSF13B, POU2F2 and SLAMF1 (Fig. 3G and H). Discussion

As the most common subtype of all renal cancers, ccRCC is highly aggressive [25]. Immune blockade and targeted therapy have shown remarkable outcomes. However, not all patients respond well to such therapies [26,27]. Therefore, it is crucial to explore novel therapeutic targets or adjuvants to enhance immune therapy for renal cancer. The occurrence of tumors is a dynamic process influenced by a wide range of factors, multiple stages and multiple genes. And the dysregulation of programmed cell death is closely correlated with the occurrence and prognosis of tumors [28]. Disulfidptosis exhibits an unusual accumulation of intracellular disulfides, which sets it apart from other forms of PCD processes. In the condition of high SLC7A11 expression, limiting NADPH synthesis from the PPP by glucose deprivation results in a significant buildup of small molecule disulfides, a number of redox abnormalities, as well as rapid cell death [11,29]. That's to say, disulfidptosis has the potential to be applied to a novel effective therapy method for ccRCC patients. Besides, a subgroup of tumor cells known as cancer stem cells (CSCs) has the ability to initiate tumors and trigger relapses. Due to the primary characteristics of cancer stem cells, it is claimed that cancer stemness is the primary reason for metastasis and tumor relapse following conventional therapy. In addition to genetic modification, TME may play a role in facilitating the acquisition of stemness characteristics [30,31]. Currently, there is limited research on the impact of disulfidoptosis-related genes on the prognosis of ccRCC. In our study, we collected 88 DRGs. Using univariate Cox regression analysis, we obtained 44 prognostic genes in ccRCC. Then, we discovered two ccRCC subtypes associated with DRG from the TCGA through the NMF algorithm. Compared to the C1 subtype, ccRCC patients in cluster 2 had a better clinical outcome, which confirmed the significance of DRGs in ccRCC prognosis. Next, differential genes related to cell stemness and TME were obtained through WGCNA analysis with the differential genes between the two clusters. And we obtained 71 cell stemness and TME associated disulfidptosis-related DEGs by taking the intersection up-expressed genes in tumor samples and hub genes in WGCNA. After univariate Cox analysis, we used LASSO regression analysis based on the expression matrix of genes highly related to outcome (p < 0.05) to establish a prognostic signature including nine genes (MYO1F, FMNL1, FCGR1B, TNFAIP8L2, CYTH4, NCF4, TNFSF13B, POU2F2 and SLAMF1) from the training set, which shown great efficiency in predicting survival outcome and immunotherapeutic response. Additionally, the disulfidptosis risk score was identified as an independent prognostic factor in ccRCC patients. Subsequently, a nomogram was developed, integrating the risk score and clinical features, to predict the survival outcome of ccRCC patients. Of the nine genes in our constructed model, MYO1F is associated with the mitochondrial network and promotes tumor growth in thyroid cancer [32]. Low expression of FMNL1 may prevent the proliferation of kidney cancer cells, which is a potential KIRC-specific neoantigen candidate for KIRC mRNA vaccine development. FCGR1B is the Fc fragment of immunoglobulin G and is related to the poor prognostic of ccRCC patients [33]. TNFAIP8L2 is highly expressed in various tumors and is related to the process of immunological homeostasis [34]. According to a recent study, TNFAIP8 may be correlated with tumor-associated microbiota in colorectal cancer [35]. CYTH4 belongs to the PSCD family, regulating protein sorting and membrane dynamics. In glioma, the overexpression of CYTH4 is a biomarker for predicting a poor prognosis by affecting pyroptosis [36]. NCF4 is a component of the NAPDH complex, correlated with immune activation and immunological regulation [37]. TNFSF13B, commonly known as BAFF, is a member of the tumor necrosis factor (TNF) ligand family. It could serve as a crucial communication channel between the tumor cell and the tumor microenvironment for KIRC [38]. As a marker of prognosis in cancer, POU2F2 is highly expressed in various solid tumors. POU2F2 has been implicated in promoting the glycolysis and progression of glioblastoma by the activation the AKT pathway [[39], [40], [41], [42]]. SLAMF1 has been described as a vital molecule in regulating autophagy in chronic lymphocytic leukemia cells. In choriocarcinoma, SLAMF1 promotes resistance to MTX by activating autophagy-related pathways [43]. These key genes' differential expression was strongly correlated with the prognosis of tumors. Further research on these molecules may provide a reference for exploring novel therapeutics and prognostic indicators for renal cancer. TME is a dynamic system and is characterized by various components, including immune cells, stromal cells and cytokines, which is not only closely correlated with the occurrence, growth and metastasis of tumors, but also vital to the treatment of tumors [44,45]. TME plays a crucial part in malignant progression, immune escape and treatment resistance. TME relies largely on CD8 T cells, which are essential for recognising and eliminating tumor cells [46]. Besides, neutrophils play a crucial role as a significant component of immune cells infiltrating the TME. Recent research has revealed that the infiltration of neutrophils plays a role in promoting lymphangiogenesis and lymph node metastasis, which was associated with poor prognosis [47,48]. Both CD8 T cell and Treg cell expression were found to be significantly higher in the high-risk group of our study. A significant number of immunosuppressive substances may be present in TME, inhibiting the antitumor activity of CD8 T cells, which may explain why the high-risk group with poor prognosis showed an abundant infiltration of CD8 T cells. Tregs are a unique subgroup of T cells, which have the function of suppressing the immune response, thereby maintaining homeostasis and self-tolerance. Inhibiting T cell growth and cytokine production, as shown by studies, is one way that Tregs help to avoid autoimmunity [49]. High Tregs infiltration was significantly correlated with shorter OS of renal cancer and breast cancer [50,51]. This may explain why we found a negative correlation between the infiltration of Tregs and prognosis in the high-risk group. M1 macrophages are considered as tumor-killing macrophages [52], and the infiltration of M1 macrophages was significantly increased in the low-risk group in our analysis. In the microenvironment of solid malignant tumors, various inflammatory cells contribute to innate and acquired immune responses. Among these cells, neutrophils constitute a significant portion. Recent evidence suggests that tumor-associated neutrophils (TAN) may exhibit either a N1 anti-tumor or N2 pro-tumor phenotype. Neutrophils can undergo a transformation into tumor-promoting cells in the microenvironment. Apart from their cytotoxic effects, they have the ability to facilitate tumor cell dissemination by releasing elastase and matrix metalloproteinases. Moreover, T cell-mediated anti-tumor immune responses are restricted by the tumor-promoting state's regulation of immunosuppression through the secretion of arginase-1 and reactive oxygen species. Targeting these tumor-promoting effects could hold promise for cancer therapy. Neutrophils also contribute to tumor promotion through the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) within the tumor, a process called NETosis. Existing evidence predominantly supports the tumor-promoting role of neutrophils, with the degree of neutrophil infiltration correlating with a poorer prognosis. In our study, we observed higher levels of neutrophil infiltration in the high-risk group. Notably, the depletion of neutrophils is related to the significant reduction in lung carcinogenesis. These findings suggest that the SADG-related risk score model may have the potential to guide personalized immunotherapy for patients with ccRCC [[53], [54], [55], [56]]. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) are gradually being suggested as first-line adjuvant treatment for diverse tumors due to their impressive therapeutic outcomes [26,57,58]. The immune checkpoint-associated genes were also found to be substantially different between the two risk groupings and to be more strongly expressed in the high-risk group, suggesting that high-risk patients may respond to ICI immunotherapy better. The TIDE approach has been applied to forecast the ICB response in ccRCC patients. TIDE score predicts the likelihood of immune evasion. According to our findings, patients in the high-risk group may be more likely to experience an immunological escape since their TIDE scores were higher than those of the low-risk group. Overall, the findings imply that the patients' different immunological states may have contributed to the differences in prognosis between the high- and low-risk groups. Although this study performed a genomics analysis of disulfidptosis, and this SADG-related risk score model showed great capacity to predict the prognosis of patients with ccRCC, several limitations should be noted. First, although the expression of nine core genes was verified in TCGA and a small number of clinical samples collected by us, prospective studies with a significant sample size and prognostic information are essential for further verification. Second, the results were obtained by bioinformatics analyses from public databases, which need to be validated by further biological evidence from experimental studies. Furthermore, the underlying mechanisms between the SADGs and the immune microenvironment in ccRCC deserve further research.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we thoroughly explored the differences among biological processes, the immune states and the survival outcomes between the two molecular subtypes of disulfidoptosis. Furthermore, we established a novel signature based on nine stemness-associated disulfidptosis genes with excellent validity and potential clinical applications. This study will improve our understanding of the function of disulfidptosis in ccRCC, which offers a new reference for identifying prognostic markers and contributes to the improvement of therapeutic approaches.
