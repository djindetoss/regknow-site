Data-efficient unsupervised deep learning deformable SPECT/CT registration framework for voxel-level radionuclide therapy dosimetry: validation using clinical 131I DTC therapy data

Abstract

To develop a data-efficient, unsupervised deep learning framework for deformable SPECT/CT registration that supports voxel-based dosimetry in radionuclide therapy, particularly in clinical settings with limited imaging datasets. We propose Nufit-Reg, an unsupervised deep learning network for deformable SPECT/CT registration. The network employs dual Swin-Transformer-based encoders to process CT and SPECT images separately, with cross-stitch units enabling structured feature sharing and multimodal integration across hierarchical stages. To overcome data scarcity, a two-stage training strategy was adopted. In the first stage, inter-patient pre-training was performed using randomly paired iodine-131 SPECT/CT scans (a total of 58 scans) to learn general anatomical correspondences. In the second stage, few-shot fine-tuning was performed using intra-patient sequential SPECT/CT pairs derived from quantitative scans (12 patients, each with three time points), enabling the model to adapt to patient-specific temporal consistency while being optimized across multiple patients. Registration performance was evaluated against Elastix and UTSRMorph using image similarity metrics, including structural similarity (SSIM), weighted SSIM (wSSIM), local normalized cross-correlation (LNCC), and mutual information (MI), as well as deformation regularity assessed via Jacobian determinant analysis. The downstream impact of registration on voxel-level time-activity-curve (TAC) fitting and tumor dose distributions (D2%, D98%) was also quantitatively analyzed. Nufit-Reg achieved a superior weighted SSIM (0.8968 ± 0.0241). It significantly outperforming Elastix and CT UTSRMorph. In dosimetry analysis, the enhanced registration accuracy led to an 11.7% reduction in tumor fitting RMSE compared to Elastix. These improvements aligned with more stable dose distributions. This was evidenced by reduced D2% extremes (110.81 to 95.1 Gy), and higher median D98% 3.30 Gy. This study introduces Nufit-Reg, a two-stage unsupervised framework that achieves accurate and efficient deformable SPECT/CT registration under data-limited conditions. By combining inter-patient pre-training with intra-patient fine-tuning, Nufit-Reg provides a practical solution for supporting individualized voxel-based dosimetry in I-131 radionuclide therapy and potentially for other radionuclide treatments in data-scarce clinical environments.

Background

Building upon the foundational success of therapies such as Iodine-131 for thyroid disease, radionuclide therapy has a clinical history spanning eight decades. More recently, this field has attracted significant clinical and research interest, particularly after the approval of novel 177Lu-based radiopharmaceuticals [1, 2]. Unlike external beam radiotherapy, radionuclide therapy achieves targeted cell killing by precisely delivering radionuclides directly to tumor cells. The deposition of high-energy emissions induces DNA damage through single- or double-strand breaks [3]. While radiopharmaceuticals offer substantial therapeutic efficacy, accurate estimation of absorbed dose is essential for establishing clinical dose–response relationships in radionuclide therapy. Such estimation is critical for evaluating therapeutic outcomes and predicting associated toxicities [4]. Utilizing sequential quantitative SPECT/CT data, voxel-level dosimetry combines anatomical and functional information. This approach offers a superior alternative to the mean-absorbed dose method by precisely accounting for patient-specific anatomical variations and heterogeneities in radiopharmaceutical uptake [5, 6]. In practice, voxel-level dosimetry involves sequential SPECT/CT acquisitions at multiple time points, followed by image registration, time-integrated activity (TIA) map generation, and dose calculation [7, 8]. Within this workflow, image registration is the most critical step for accurate activity quantification, as misregistration prevents the precise spatial alignment of serial SPECT/CT scans and leads to erroneous voxel-wise kinetic curve fitting during TIA estimation. These errors directly propagate into the final dose calculation, since the TIA map is the foundational input for dose kernel convolution or Monte Carlo simulations. Previous studies have shown that even relatively small registration errors, corresponding to translations below 9.5 mm and rotations below 6°, can result in absorbed dose deviations of up to 90% [9]. However, direct registration based on SPECT intensities is often challenging due to tracer kinetics, limited spatial resolution and low signal-to-noise ratios. In hybrid SPECT/CT systems, CT provides a relatively stable anatomical reference that can support spatial alignment across time points. As SPECT and CT are acquired in a co-registered framework, anatomical consistency in CT space can serve as an indirect constraint for maintaining the spatial consistency of functional distributions. Deep learning has become a widely used approach in medical image analysis, particularly for image registration. By learning deformation fields directly from image pairs, deep learning-based registration achieves competitive accuracy but at much faster inference than traditional optimization-based approaches [10, 11]. However, supervised deep learning relies on true deformation labels which are often difficult or impractical to obtain. To overcome this, unsupervised learning has been introduced using an image-driven loss function that minimizes image dissimilarity and add regularization. This lets deep learning models train efficiently on unlabeled data while maintaining their speed and accuracy [12, 13]. Examples of fast, accurate unsupervised models include DIRnet [14], VoxelMorph [11] and TransMorph [15]. Though effective for single modality registration (e.g., CT-CT or MR-MR), their application in hybrid SPECT/CT registration remains limited. Recently, an unsupervised SPECT/CT registration approach used a large-scale Lutetium-177 DOTATATE therapy dataset from two medical centers [16]. However, this key dataset is not universally accessible. The lack of publicly available large-scale datasets for sequential SPECT/CTs [17, 18] has forced many institutions to rely on their own small internal data, which was often limited to 10–20 patients, especially during the early stages of radionuclide therapy implementation. Consequently, institutions with limited patient data face substantial challenges, which can hinder the adoption of network-based deformable registration for clinical dosimetry. To address these limitations, we propose Nufit-Reg, a novel two-stage unsupervised deformable registration framework. Nufit-Reg is specifically designed for data-scarce clinical environments. The core novelty lies in its training strategy, which combines inter-patient pre-training with intra-patient few-shot fine-tuning on sequential SPECT/CT scans. This design enables the model to learn generalized deformation patterns while capturing intra-patient temporal variability, ensuring reliable deformable registration even with small patient datasets. By achieving accurate voxel-level spatial correspondence, Nufit-Reg facilitates reliable TIA calculation, thereby addressing a critical data scarcity gap in nuclear medicine and supporting personalized treatment planning in radionuclide therapy.

Materials and methods › Data acquisition

All data for this study were retrospectively collected from a single institution (The First Affiliated Hospital of USTC). All patients were inpatients with thyroid cancer receiving 100 mCi oral I-131 therapy at the Department of Nuclear Medicine. The dataset comprised two distinct subsets: a pre-training dataset (DPre) of 58 patients, used for inter-patient primary training to learn generalized anatomical deformation patterns, where all SPECT/CT data were acquired approximately 96 h post-administration; and a few-shot tuning dataset (DTuning) of 12 patients. 12 patients underwent three time-point quantitative SPECT/CT scans at approximately 30, 54, and 78 h post-administration. All enrolled patients met the following criteria: 1) between 18 and 60 years old; 2) completion of total or near-total thyroidectomy; 3) pathological confirmation of differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC); and 4) indication for I-131 remnant ablation therapy with 100 mCi oral free iodine, while excluding patients with distant metastases. Materials and methods › Data preprocessing

For all acquired SPECT/CT image pairs, the initial processing steps ensured uniformity and precise spatial correspondence across the dataset, addressing inconsistencies in the native fields of view (FOV) and resolutions between the two modalities. Specifically, the overlapping physical imaging range was determined, and both CT and SPECT images were cropped accordingly to ensure that the resulting image volumes covered the identical anatomical region and shared the same physical coordinate system. During this process, the spatial metadata from the original DICOM headers, including image origin and orientation, were strictly preserved to maintain a shared physical coordinate system. Next, the image intensity values underwent normalization. CT images were clipped to the range of – 1000–2000 HU and subsequently normalized to [0, 1]. SPECT images were normalized to the range [0,1] using min–max scaling to account for differences in voxel intensity units across datasets. Following intensity normalization, all images were resampled to a consistent 256 × 256 × 128 voxel grid using linear interpolation. This strategy effectively standardized the voxel spacing while preserving the quantitative integrity of SPECT intensities by avoiding high-order artifacts. Finally, an initial rigid registration was performed using Elastix with its default rigid registration parameter configuration on the CT pair. To maintain spatial alignment, the resulting transformation matrix was simultaneously applied to the corresponding SPECT images using linear interpolation. This step ensures global alignment across both modalities before the deformable stage.

Materials and methods › Network architecture

Illustrated in Fig. 1, Nufit-Reg is an unsupervised deep learning framework for deformable registration of sequential SPECT/CTs. Two independent encoders process SPECT and CT images separately, while a single decoder generates the 3D deformation field for registration. Each encoder takes a two-channel volume as input, representing the fixed and moving images of the respective modality, with an initial size of 256 × 256 × 128. The number of feature channels is set to 64 in the first layer and is doubled with each subsequent hierarchical stage to capture increasingly complex deep features. The backbone consists of four hierarchical stages, each comprising a set of Swin Transformer (ST) blocks [19]. To enhance feature extraction for complex anatomical and functional patterns, we adopted Fusion Attention Blocks (FAB) and an Overlapping Attention Block (OAB) from prior work [20]. The FAB synergizes local and global tokens, leveraging convolutional operations to reduce local redundancy [21] while employing self-attention mechanisms to capture long-range spatial dependencies [22]. Simultaneously, the OAB facilitates cross-window information exchange through overlapping windows, thereby enhancing the network’s representative capacity [23–25]. Within these blocks, the self-attention mechanism is computed within local sub-volumes, using a 3D window size of 7 × 6x5 to capture spatial dependencies while maintaining computational efficiency [20]. To achieve structured multi-modal feature integration, the network utilizes Cross-stitch Units (CSU), a feature-sharing mechanism previously adopted in multi-task and multi-modal learning frameworks [16, 26]. These units are strategically placed at the first two hierarchical stages and the deepest bottleneck layer to enable soft parameter sharing between SPECT and CT feature representations through learnable linear combinations of feature activations. In the decoding phase, continuous Super-Resolution (SR) upsampling layers are utilized to replace traditional interpolation and skip-connection operations. By mapping latent features into a continuous coordinate-based representation, these SR layers effectively mitigate image degradation incurred during the encoding process and reconstruct high-fidelity spatial correspondences within the displacement field [27–29].Fig. 1The network structure of Nufit-Reg showing the dual-encoder architecture for separate CT and SPECT feature extraction, cross-stitch units for multimodal feature interaction, and a shared decoder for predicting the three-dimensional deformation field The network structure of Nufit-Reg showing the dual-encoder architecture for separate CT and SPECT feature extraction, cross-stitch units for multimodal feature interaction, and a shared decoder for predicting the three-dimensional deformation field

Materials and methods › Training strategy

Given the limited size of available SPECT/CT dataset, directly training a registration model on the target dataset (DTuning) alone may lead to suboptimal generalization. To mitigate this limitation, a two-stage training strategy was adopted, consisting of an inter-patient pre-training stage followed by an intra-patient few-shot fine-tuning stage. This strategy allows the model to first learn general geometric priors from a larger retrospective dataset and subsequently adapt to the target-domain data. In the inter-patient pre-training stage (Stage 1), the model was initialized using a large-scale dataset, DPre (58 patients). To ensure learning from morphologically congruent data, 150 inter-patient image pairs were generated based on tracheal spatial geometry. Specifically, three spatial parameters were extracted: the distances from the superior and inferior ends of the trachea to the respective CT volume boundaries, and the total tracheal length. The trachea was designated as a stable longitudinal reference axis due to its high radiographic contrast and structural stability against physiological deformation. Using these raw geometric features, the standard Euclidean distance was computed between all possible patient pairs. Ultimately, the 150 pairs exhibiting the minimum Euclidean distances were chosen to construct the paired pre-training dataset. These pairs were randomly split into training (n = 130) and validation (n = 20) sets to establish a robust parameter foundation. In the subsequent adaptive fine-tuning stage (Stage 2), the pre-trained model was transferred to the target dataset DTuning (12 patients, 36 intra-patient registration pairs). To rigorously evaluate the model’s clinical generalizability, we implemented five independent patient-level evaluation sessions. In each session, the test set consisted of all three registration pairs from a single patient, which was entirely excluded from that session’s training (n = 27) and validation (n = 6) sets to ensure the target anatomy remained completely "unseen". The reported results represent the average performance across these independent sessions. This rotation-based testing strategy ensures that the framework’s performance is validated across multiple independent patient under varying training data distributions, thereby providing a comprehensive and robust assessment of its adaptation capability to intra-patient registration tasks. To prevent overfitting and enhance the robustness of the few-shot model, online data augmentation was applied in Stage 2. This involved a random combination of three affine deformations: ± 5° rotation, ± 5% translation, and ± 3% scaling. This strategy effectively simulated data variability, maximizing the utility of the limited DTuning for domain adaptation. The training was performed on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 24 GB GPU.

Materials and methods › Loss functions

The unsupervised optimization objective of Nufit-Reg is to minimize a composite loss function that ensures anatomically plausible transformations while maintaining topological integrity. We adopt a decoupled strategy where high-resolution CT serves as a stable anatomical proxy. SPECT-based similarity terms are intentionally excluded to avoid coupling the registration process with tracer-dependent intensity variations, including physiological kinetics and low signal-to-noise ratios, which may lead to unstable optimization and non-physical deformations. Instead, the framework relies on CT-driven structural constraints to guide deformation. This design leverages the intrinsic co-registration between CT and SPECT in hybrid imaging systems. Materials and methods › Evaluation metrics

To evaluate the registration performance of Nufit-Reg, we compared it with Elastix [32] and UTSRMorph [20]. For Elastix, we used a B-spline deformable registration framework with a multi-resolution optimization strategy consisting of four pyramid levels. The similarity metric was Advanced Mattes Mutual Information, optimized using Adaptive Stochastic Gradient Descent. The B-spline transform was configured with a final control point spacing of 16 mm in physical space, which controls the flexibility of the deformation field. A random sampling strategy with 2048 spatial samples per iteration was used to efficiently estimate of the similarity metric. The maximum number of iterations was set to 1000. Linear B-spline interpolation was used during optimization, and cubic interpolation was applied to generate the final deformation field. UTSRMorph was implemented using its original architecture with 64 initial feature channels and trained under the same loss formulation as Nufit-Reg, using CT-only inputs. Registration accuracy was quantified using SSIM, MI, and Local Normalized Cross-Correlation (LNCC) of CT images. To evaluate the impact of registration on dosimetry, voxel-based tumor dose distributions were generated via GATE (v9.2) Monte Carlo simulations [35].

Results › Overall performance and baseline comparison

The quantitative results in Table 2 show that all registration methods significantly improve image similarity over the unregistered inputs. Compared with Elastix, Nufit-Reg achieved superior performance across SSIM, wSSIM, and LNCC (p < 0.05), with effect sizes characterized as Large to Very Large. Although the MI difference was not statistically significant, a Medium effect size (d = 0.58) was observed. Compared with UTSRMorph, Nufit-Reg showed higher values in wSSIM (p < 0.001) and MI (p = 0.0026). The effect size for the MI improvement was 1.06, while the gain in wSSIM reached an effect size of 0.38. In SSIM and LNCC, numerical differences between the two learning-based methods did not reach statistical significance, with recorded effect sizes in the Small to Medium range. Visual assessment (Fig. 3) corroborates these findings, showing that Nufit-Reg provided the most refined spatial alignment, particularly in addressing the Y-axis misregistration seen in Elastix. Results › Deformation field topology and regularity

Jacobian determinant statistics for Elastix, UTSRMorph, and Nufit-Reg are summarized in Table 4. For all methods, the proportion of voxels with negative Jacobian determinants was very low, below 1 × 10–4, indicating an absence of substantial folding. Among the compared methods, Nufit-Reg achieved the lowest fold ratio at 3.85 × 10–5. Both learning-based approaches exhibited more extreme minimum and maximum Jacobian determinant values than Elastix. These localized extreme values are primarily observed at the beginning and end slices along the z-axis, as well as in regions with large anatomical displacements such as the upper limbs. These areas typically involve less consistent structural correspondence or greater motion variability, allowing the model more flexibility to accommodate spatial misalignment. Despite these localized extreme values, the mean Jacobian determinant values for UTSRMorph and Nufit-Reg were close to unity at 0.9970 and 0.9974, respectively, compared with 0.9739 for Elastix. Discussion

To enhance voxel-based dosimetry accuracy, Kim et al. proposed a Swin Transformer-based unsupervised registration framework, CoRX-NET [16], which performs registration in a single-stage training process, where CT and SPECT images are jointly used to compute similarity losses and directly optimize the deformation field in an end-to-end manner. Building on this backbone, the proposed Nufit-Reg introduces both architectural and training modifications. First, OAB and FAB modules are incorporated to enhance multi-scale feature representation and cross-scale interaction, enabling more effective modeling of complex deformation patterns in sequential SPECT/CT registration. Consistent with the ablation results, removing these components resulted in decreased registration performance, suggesting their contribution to deformation modeling across different spatial scales. In addition, the performance decrease observed in the w/o CSU variant suggests that soft feature sharing between SPECT and CT representations may contribute to improved multi-modal spatial correspondence. The two-stage strategy effectively addresses the challenge of limited clinical data by providing a stable initialization (Stage 1), which improves optimization stability for the subsequent longitudinal refinement (Stage 2). As validated by our ablation studies, removing Stage 1 pre-training resulted in decreased accuracy and increased inter-subject variability, whereas Stage 2 fine-tuning consistently improved all evaluation metrics (p < 0.01). Quantitative analysis across the patient cohort showed that Nufit-Reg outperformed both Elastix and UTSRMorph in image similarity metrics. Notably, Nufit-Reg achieved a weighted SSIM (wSSIM) of 0.8923 ± 0.0314, demonstrating significant gains over Elastix (0.8678 ± 0.0294, d = 0.81) and UTSRMorph (0.8811 ± 0.0267, d = 0.38). Given that wSSIM inherently weights high-activity regions more heavily, this numerical increment suggests improved spatial alignment within these clinically relevant high-uptake zones, where even minor misalignments typically incur substantial penalties in the metric. This indicates that the framework effectively preserves the spatial fidelity of high-tracer-uptake regions. Improved registration in high-uptake regions is critical for TIA estimation in radionuclide therapy. Nufit-Reg achieved the highest mean R2 values and the lowest mean RMSE across tumor voxels, suggesting improved overall fitting accuracy. Voxel-level distribution analysis further indicated a reduction in poorly fitted voxels with low R2 values and fewer extreme fitting errors. Accordingly, the changes in Table 6 appear to reflect a more balanced and spatially coherent dose distribution, as supported by the decrease in D2% and increase in D98%. With respect to dosimetric estimation, a contraction of the TIA distribution range and a reduction in mean dose were observed. Crucially, because baseline tracer uptake varies drastically across individual patients, cohort-level median metrics can obscure significant patient-specific dosimetric shifts. For instance, in the patient with the highest baseline uptake, Nufit-Reg induced an absolute mean dose shift of nearly 3 Gy (from 28.74 to 25.83 Gy) compared to Elastix, demonstrating that the dosimetric impact of registration accuracy becomes particularly substantial for highly sensitive targets. These findings suggest that the observed dose differences are more likely associated with improved registration consistency than with systematical tracer underestimation. By design, this behavior aligns with our optimization strategy, which relies exclusively on CT-based structural similarity rather than SPECT-based intensity loss. Unlike CoRX-NET that enforce joint SPECT/CT alignment during training, our model avoids the direct manipulation of functional signals. Consequently, the gains in voxel-level dosimetry should be interpreted as an indirect dividend of refined spatial correspondence guided by anatomical anchors. Admittedly, the absence of direct functional constraints during training makes it challenging to definitively decouple true functional correspondence from the secondary gains of structural registration. However, this anatomy-driven framework is robustly supported by quantitative phantom dosimetric validations. Using digital phantoms with an absolute ground truth, Ao et al. [36] showed that non-rigid registration drastically reduces dosimetric errors (e.g., − 15.5 to − 2.1%). However, direct functional alignment remains clinically unreliable due to low spatial resolution and dynamic tracer redistribution. Using the same phantom, Li et al. [37]showed that CT-guided registration consistently outperforms SPECT alignment, substantially reducing dosimetric errors in the liver (− 2.51 vs. − 8.66%) and spleen (− 3.60 vs. − 14.81%). Thus, high-resolution CT boundaries offer a far more robust regularization for deformation fields than low-resolution functional signals. Finally, these metrics serve as proxies for dosimetric reliability rather than direct proof of accuracy, as a high-resolution ground truth for absorbed dose is unavailable. While Nufit-Reg improves the technical robustness and biokinetic consistency of the dosimetry pipeline, its direct impact on clinical outcomes should be interpreted with caution. Despite the encouraging performance, several limitations should be noted. First, the evaluation was performed on a relatively small, single-center cohort, which may limit the generalizability to other tracers and clinical settings. Second, as discussed, the inherent lack of an in vivo high-resolution dosimetric ground truth restricts our evaluation to metrics of spatial and biokinetic consistency rather than absolute accuracy. Future studies utilizing high-resolution dose ground truth or multi-center datasets are needed to further validate the clinical impact of these technical improvements.

Conclusion

In this study, we proposed a data-efficient, unsupervised deformable SPECT/CT registration framework for sequential quantitative SPECT/CT images. Evaluation on an I-131 SPECT/CT dataset demonstrated the potential of Nufit-Reg to enhance the spatial alignment robustness of voxel-level dosimetry. By combining inter-patient pre-training with intra-patient few-shot fine-tuning, Nufit-Reg achieved improved performance compared with conventional B-spline-based Elastix and UTSR in CT-based registration metrics, including SSIM, LNCC, and MI, even with a limited dataset. Furthermore, a preliminary analysis of dose-volume metrics, including D2% and D98%, suggests that the improved registration consistency contributes to more physically plausible dose distributions. While these results indicate that Nufit-Reg provides a robust technical approach for deformable registration, further validation in larger, multi-center cohorts is necessary to establish its direct impact on clinical decision-making. Overall, this framework serves as a supportive tool for improving the reliability of the internal dosimetry pipeline.
