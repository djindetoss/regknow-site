Abstract

This study addresses the critical issue of drug-induced torsades de pointes (TdP) risk assessment, a vital aspect of new drug development due to its association with arrhythmia and sudden cardiac death. Existing methodologies, particularly those reliant on a single biomarker derived from CiPA O'Hara-Rudy (CiPAORdv1.0) ventricular cell model without the hERG dynamic as input to the individual machine learning model, have limitations in capturing the complexity inherent in the comprehensive range of factors influencing drug-induced TdP risk. This study aims to overcome these limitations by proposing a stacking ensemble machine learning approach by integrating multiple in silico biomarkers derived from the CiPAORdv1.0 with hERG dynamic characteristics. The ensemble machine learning model consisted of three artificial neural network (ANN) models as baseline model and support vector machine (SVM), logistic regression (LR), random forest (RF), and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) models as meta-classifier. The highest AUC score of 1.00 (0.90-1.00) for high risk, 0.97 (0.84-1.00) for intermediate risk, and 1.00 (0.87-1.00) for low risk were obtained using seven biomarkers derived from the CiPAORdv1.0 with hERG dynamic characteristics. Furthering our investigation, we explored the model's robustness by incorporating interindividual variability into the generation of in silico biomarkers from a population of human ventricular cell models. This study also enabled an analysis of TdP risk classification under high clinical exposure and therapeutic scenarios for several drugs. Additionally, from a sensitivity analysis, we revealed four important ion channels, namely, CaL, NaL, Na, and Kr channels that affect significantly the important biomarkers for TdP risk prediction.

INTRODUCTION

Evaluating a drug's cardiac safety concerning torsades de pointes (TdP) risk is essential in developing a new drug. 1 TdP risk is associated with drug‐induced arrhythmia as a critical indicator of sudden cardiac death events. 2 The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) introducing comprehensive in vitro proarrhythmia assay (CiPA) studies, which assess drug responses across multiple ion channels, offering an alternative to the conventional single‐assay approach that solely utilizes the hERG channel. 3 , 4 Several studies followed the CiPA guidelines to develop an evaluation system for predicting the TdP risk of drugs using in silico biomarkers derived from the CiPAORdv1.0 ventricular cell model. The study proposed by Lancaster et al. 5 reported the highest performance with an area under curve (AUC) score of 0.962 by utilizing action potential duration of 50% repolarization (APD50) and Ca2+ to the support vector machine (SVM). Yoo et al. 6 utilized nine in silico biomarkers derived from CiPAORdv1.0 without hERG dynamic as input to the artificial neural networks (ANN) model for predicting the TdP risk of drugs into the high, intermediate, and low‐risk levels. Li et al. 7 introduced hERG dynamic pharmacological interactions of drugs, and Duta et al. 8 added some model optimizations by revising the maximum conductance of some major ionic currents, including IKs, ICaL, IKr, INaL, and IK1 to 1.870, 1.007, 1.013, 2.661, and 1.698, respectively, to the O'Hara‐Rudy (ORd) model. 9 To categorize cardiotoxicity risk groups, the FDA employed qNet—the total net charge transported over the six channels, INaL, ICaL, IKr, Ito, IK1, and IKs—as a biomarker. 8 , 10 Furthermore, Li et al. 11 incorporated the ordinal logistic regression (OLR) and 10,000‐time testing to evaluate the performance of the machine learning algorithm to predict the TdP risk of drugs. Their study showed that the AUC score was higher for drugs that incorporated the hERG dynamic in classifying the TdP risk of drugs than those that did not utilize the hERG dynamic model. Previous studies proposed a single biomarker, such as qNet from the CiPAORdv1.0 ventricular cell model that strongly correlates with TdP risk, particularly in predicting high and low‐risk levels. 11 However, other research indicated that individual biomarkers, such as APD50, calcium transient duration of 50% repolarization (CaD50), and qNet did not effectively classify drugs into high, intermediate, and low‐risk categories. 12 , 13 This is possibly because a single biomarker cannot encompass the full range of factors contributing to a drug's TdP risk. To address the critical gap from the previous studies, this study proposes a novel approach by incorporating hERG dynamic characteristics and employing a stacking ensemble machine learning model. This model synergizes multiple in silico biomarkers derived from the CiPAORdv1.0 enhanced with hERG dynamics to improve the performance of drug‐induced TdP risk assessment. The ensemble approach combines the strengths of three ANN models as the baseline with SVM, LR, random forest (RF), and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) models as meta‐classifiers. Through this comprehensive methodology, we aim to address the limitations of previous models by providing a more accurate, comprehensive, and nuanced risk assessment across high, intermediate, and low TdP risk categories. Furthermore, recognizing the importance of interindividual variability in drug response, our study extends the application of these models to account for physiological differences within the population. By generating in silico biomarkers from a diverse human ventricular cell model population. This approach is further validated by evaluating TdP risk classification for several drugs under conditions of high clinical exposure and therapeutic scenarios proposed by Leishman et al., 14 underscoring the practical utility of our model in predicting TdP risk in a clinical setting. In addition, we conduct a sensitivity analysis to explore the influence of specific ion channel balances on in silico biomarkers for TdP risk of drug classification. This analysis reinforces the importance of hERG dynamics in accurately predicting TdP risk and sheds light on the intricate relationship between ion channel dynamics and drug‐induced cardiotoxicity. Through this comprehensive methodology, our study aims to advance the field of cardiac safety pharmacology, offering a robust and clinically relevant tool for predicting TdP risk, thereby facilitating safer drug development and deployment.

METHODS

A whole schematic of a stacking ensemble machine learning model with fivefold cross‐validation for predicting cardiac toxicity of drugs into high, intermediate, and low risk using in silico biomarkers.

METHODS › In vitro dataset for assessing cardiac toxicity of drug

We utilized FDA experimental data of 28 drugs obtained from Li et al. 11 These 28 drugs are divided according to the CiPA into 12 drugs of the training dataset and 16 drugs of the testing dataset, as detailed in Table S1. The dataset included measuring inhibition rates for seven ion channels in response to four different concentration levels of the 28 drugs. The four ion channels include sodium current (INa), late sodium current (INaL), rapid delayed rectifier potassium current (IKr), and L‐type calcium current (ICaL). To ensure consistency, we processed all the datasets by the methodology proposed by Crumb et al. 15 Initially, we assessed the uncertainty associated with the in vitro dataset using the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) technique introduced by Chang et al. 10 which involved generating 2000 Hill curves within a 95% confidence interval. Subsequently, we derived the half‐maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) and the slope coefficients at IC50 (Hill coefficients, h) from these 2000 Hill curves. These IC50 and h values were then employed as inputs to the in silico drug simulations.

METHODS › In silico simulation for generating biomarkers

For in silico simulations, we applied the CiPAORdv1.0 model, a modified cardiac cell model from the original ORd model that was later modified by Li et al. 7 (incorporating the dynamic inhibition model of hERG channel) and refined by Dutta et al. 8 The typical mathematical expression of membrane potential (Vm) can be written as shown in Equation (1): where the cm is the cell membrane capacitance, the Istim is the stimulus current, and the Itotal is the summation of transmembrane ionic currents. For the CiPAORdv1.0 ventricular cell model, the Itotal is assumed to consist of the sodium current INa, the late sodium current INaL, the L‐type calcium current ICaL, the sodium current through L‐type calcium channel ICaNa, the potassium current through L‐type calcium channel ICaK, the transient outward potassium current Ito, inward rectifier potassium current IKs, sodium‐calcium exchange current INaCa, sodium‐potassium ATPase current INaK, background currents INabICabIKb, and sarcolemma calcium pump current IpCa. The in silico simulations utilized the endocardial cell type. The drug inhibition model for hERG and non‐hERG channels is described in Supplementary Materials.

METHODS › Ensemble machine learning with stacking algorithm

A stacking ensemble machine learning algorithm combines several models as baseline models to provide a superior meta‐learning classifier with a more accurate classification, reducing overfitting and building a robust classifier system. 16 , 17 , 18 Generally, a stacking algorithm consists of two levels of classifier layers: level 1 and level 2. The level 1 classifier layer has several classifier models as baseline models. 18 Meanwhile, the level 2 classifier layer consists of a single model as a meta‐classifier. In ensemble machine learning, implementing k‐fold cross‐validation is essential to ensure the reliability and robustness of the stacking ensemble algorithm. 19 , 20 Therefore, this study applied fivefold cross‐validation on the training and validation process. Initially, the dataset S=xiyi=1k is partitioned into k subsets S=S1S2…Sk, k−1 subsets will be used as train datasets, and the remaining subset will be used as validation datasets. 17 , 21 The training and validation performance results, as shown in Table S3, will be used as internal validation to determine the best model for the level 1 classifier. The level 1 classifier layers involve several classifier models (M1, M2,…Mn) that will generate the output M1 (xi) and M2 xi until Mn (xi), respectively. Furthermore, the prediction from each model will be used as input to the meta‐classifier at level 2 to determine the final prediction of cardiac toxicity of drugs. As shown in Figure 1, this study used three ANN models as baseline models and applied several models, including SVM, LR, XGBoost, and RF, model models as meta‐classifiers. RESULTS

The result of electrophysiological simulations of the cell model using CiPAORdv1.0 with hERG dynamic characteristics are 11 in silico biomarkers that are utilized as input to a stacking ensemble machine learning model. Nevertheless, we conducted biomarker selection to eliminate less impactful biomarkers, ultimately identifying four groups of in silico biomarkers comprising eleven‐, seven‐, five‐, and three‐biomarker groups. This categorization was based on the significance ranking derived from the SHAP value, as shown in Figure 2. The set of 11 in silico biomarkers group involves APD50, APD90, dVmdtrepol, dVmdtmax, Vmpeak, Vmresting, CaD50, CaD90, Capeak, Caresting, and qNet. The seven‐biomarker group involves APD50, APD90, dVmdtmax, Vmpeak, CaD50, CaD90, and qNet. The five‐biomarker group involves APD50, APD90, CaD50, CaD90, and qNet. The three‐biomarker group involves APD90, CaD90, and qNet. We evaluated the performance of a stacking ensemble machine learning model using each biomarker group generated from the CiPAORdv1.0 ventricular cell model with hERG dynamic characteristics. 15 , 22 The results of several meta‐classifiers for the proposed stacking ensemble machine learning model are presented in Table S4. According to these results, the SVM meta‐classifier achieved the highest performance compared with LR, RF, and XGBoost. Consequently, the performances presented in Tables 1 and 2 are those of the ensemble machine learning model utilizing SVM as the meta‐classifier. As shown in Table 1, the performance of the ensemble machine learning model varies across different biomarker groups and C max conditions. Under CiPA‐based C max conditions, the model achieves its highest performance with the seven‐biomarker group, achieving an AUC of 1.00 (0.90–1.00) for high risk, 0.97 (0.84–1.00) for intermediate risk, and 1.00 (0.87–1.00) for low risk. These scores indicate excellent performance according to the CiPA evaluation standard (see Table S6). The seven‐biomarker group's results outperform those of the three‐ and five‐biomarker groups, which exhibit lower AUC scores. Interestingly, the 11‐biomarker group, while comprehensive, does not match the AUC score of the seven‐biomarkers set. This suggests that adding more than the optimal seven biomarkers does not necessarily enhance predictive performance. The proposed method in this study is further confirmed by assessing TdP risk classification for several drugs, including terfenadine, astemizole, and risperidone, under conditions of high clinical exposure and therapeutic scenarios as proposed by Leishman et al. (see Table S2) highlighting the practical value of our model in forecasting TdP risk in clinical environments. Under high clinical exposure conditions, the ensemble machine learning model demonstrates a nuanced predictive performance when categorizing drugs into high, intermediate, and low TdP risk levels, as shown by AUC and accuracy metrics. Notably, within this clinical context, the group of five biomarkers yields an AUC of 0.96 (0.92–1.00) for high risk, AUC of 0.94 (0.86–0.98) for intermediate risk, and AUC of 1.00 (0.95–1.00) for low risk. In therapeutic scenarios, the ensemble machine learning model, employing a seven‐biomarker configuration, exhibits notable proficiency in identifying drugs at high risk for TdP, achieving an AUC score of 0.98 (0.88–1.00). Meanwhile, intermediate and low TdP risk was obtained at 0.94 (0.84–1.00) and 0.91 (0.88–1.00), highlighting the utility of the seven‐biomarkers set as a potential predictor for TdP risk. Such efficacy underscores the model's potential to aid therapeutic decision‐making, particularly in assessing cardiotoxicity risks. Table 2 reported the performance of the proposed ensemble machine learning model compared with prior studies. Li et al. 11 employed the qNet biomarker from the CiPAORdv1.0 ventricular cell model as input for ordinal LR, with and without considering hERG dynamic characteristics. On the other hand, Yoo et al. utilized nine in silico biomarkers from the CiPAORdv1.0 without hERG dynamic as input for the ANN model. Our proposed ensemble machine learning model, which uses seven in silico biomarkers derived from CiPAORdv1.0 incorporating hERG dynamic characteristics, achieved outstanding AUC scores and outperformed the results reported by Yoo et al. and Li et al. 6 , 11 Notably, our results are comparable with those of Li et al., who also considered hERG dynamics, achieving an AUC score of 1.00 (0.90–1.00) for high TdP risk compared with their 0.988 (0.95–1.0). In assessing the intermediate‐risk group, our model demonstrated superiority with an AUC score of 0.97 (0.84–1.00), outperforming Yoo et al., who reported an AUC of 0.83 (0.73–0.91). For the low‐risk category, our model achieved an AUC of 1.00 (0.87–1.00), surpassing both Li et al. and Yoo et al., who reported AUC scores of 0.901 (0.88–0.93) and 0.98 (0.91–1.0), respectively. Table 3 presents the results of our stacking ensemble machine learning model, designed to classify drugs by their TdP risk while incorporating interindividual variability. This variability is addressed using in silico biomarkers derived from a human ventricular cell model population. This table depicts a comparison of performance metrics for various meta‐classifiers, including SVM, LR, XGBoost, and RF, across different testing conditions: CiPA, High Clinical, and Therapeutic scenarios. In the context of CiPA, the SVM meta‐classifier exhibits performance comparable to the study by Passini et al., 23 which reported an accuracy of 96%, a sensitivity of 100%, and a specificity of 92% in classifying high and no TdP risk. Our study achieves a similar accuracy of 0.96 (0.91–1.00), an AUC score of 0.99 (0.91–1.00), a sensitivity of 0.92 (0.80–1.00), and a specificity of 0.92 (0.80–1.00). Under more demanding high clinical and therapeutic conditions, where variability and complexity are heightened, the ensemble model performs strongly. DISCUSSION

Our study explored the application of in silico biomarkers and ensemble machine learning models to assess cardiac toxicity risks. Specifically, we focused on the CiPAORdv1.0 ventricular cell model with hERG dynamic characteristics. 15 , 22 The ORd model concentrates on repolarization and shows discrepancies with human ventricular data in action potential plateau, adaptation to heart rate, and response to sodium block. Ignoring the dynamics of channel pharmacology, especially for evaluating the TdP risk of drugs that are known to interact with ion channels, would provide an incomplete picture of a drug's effect on cardiac cells. Simulating these dynamics is crucial for comprehensively assessing a drug's cardiac safety profile. The hERG channel dynamics significantly determine the action potential duration (APD) in ventricular cardiomyocytes. Including these dynamics in computational models enhances their predictive accuracy for how a drug will affect cardiac repolarization and, thus, the risk of torsades de pointes (TdP). We derived 11 in silico biomarkers from the CiPAORdv1.0 ventricular cell model with hERG dynamic characteristics. By selecting the optimal set among these biomarkers for a stacking ensemble machine learning model, we aimed to enhance the predictive capabilities for cardiac toxicity risk. Our findings, presented in Tables 1 and 2, provide valuable insights into the effectiveness of this approach. show that a seven‐biomarker group achieves optimal balance, by achieving an AUC score of 1.00 (0.90–1.00) for high risk, 0.97 (0.84–1.00) for intermediate risk, and 1.00 (0.87–1.00) for low risk, aligning with CiPA standards. This approach outperforms the three, five, and eleven‐biomarker configurations, demonstrating the utility of machine learning in biomarker selection for clinical application. When analyzing the ranking based on SHAP values of the top seven most influential biomarkers for TdP risk prediction (Figure 2) and the impact of ion channel variations on these biomarkers (Figure 3), it becomes apparent that certain key ion channels, including CaL, Kr, NaL, and Na, play a significant role in modifying TdP risk predictions. Variations in these four ion channels have a substantial effect on the changes in qNet, consistent with findings from Chang et al. 10 Similar to qNet, the APD (APD50 and APD90) and CaD (CaD50 and CaD90) display high SHAP values across three baseline ANN models, suggesting they are crucial for TdP risk classification. This aligns with Lancaster et al. 5 who found that APD50 and diastolic calcium concentration are significant biomarkers for predicting drug‐induced TdP risk. Additionally, among these channels, CaL and Na channels significantly influence Vmpeak and dVmdtmax, which are also key biomarkers for TdP risk prediction. Therefore, enhancing in vitro drug inhibition experiments targeting these four ion channels could be essential for improving overall TdP risk prediction accuracy. Furthermore, as shown in Figure 4, the variation in biomarkers toward higher drug concentrations indicates a crucial point: the choice of drug concentration where the biomarker is collected for the input to the machine learning models may affect the classification performance as different ranges of drug concentration can yield varied order of drug risk level according to the biomarker values (as previously proposed by Li et al. by using qNet as the sole biomarker for ranking the TdP risk of drugs). 11 According to Chang et al., 10 the range of drug concentrations used to evaluate the TdP risk of drugs is from 1 to 4× C max as most experimental data is available within this range. Therefore, biomarker data from outside the 1 to 4× C max can inherit high uncertainties and should not be used for analysis. Moreover, relying on a single biomarker is less accurate due to variability. Multiple biomarkers and advanced machine learning (ML) techniques improve classification, integrating diverse inputs for better TdP risk prediction by capturing complex factors affecting drug safety. The robustness of our proposed model is demonstrated by its excellent performance, even when the roles of the training and testing drugs are interchanged. According to CiPA evaluation standards, we initially used 12 drugs for training and 16 for testing. The model also showed superior performance when the configuration was reversed, with 16 drugs used for training and 12 for testing, as detailed in Tables S4 and S5. However, CiPA guidelines recommend using more testing drugs than training drugs to evaluate better the robustness and generalization ability of a ML model. In high clinical exposure and therapeutic scenarios, the consistently high AUC values across all biomarker groups for high, intermediate, and low TdP risk categorization highlight the model's potential as a reliable tool for TdP risk assessment. Its adaptability to fluctuations in drug concentrations is critical to ensuring patient safety. Our model demonstrates an excellent performance in predicting TdP risk under high clinical and therapeutic according to the CiPA evaluation standard as shown in Table S6. Moreover, using a diverse human ventricular cell model population to capture interindividual variability strengthens its applicability to real‐world clinical settings. Although this study presents new findings and demonstrates improved performance in predicting the TdP risk of drugs, it has several limitations. First, the study focused solely on one cardiac cell model. Future work could consider another model, such as the one proposed by Tomek et al. The ToR‐ORd model, developed to address specific limitations of the ORd model, offers a more consistent depiction of human ventricular myocardial cells. Using the ToR‐ORd model, the biomarkers derived would more accurately reflect physiological responses, leading to better predictive performance of the stacking ensemble ML approach and increased robustness in TdP risk classification. Second, the study relies on single‐cell electrophysiological simulations, which may not provide as comprehensive insights as tissue‐ or organ‐level simulations. A critical biomarker for TdP risk assessment is the QT interval, with significant prolongation indicating higher TdP risk. In silico QT intervals can be obtained through tissue‐ or organ‐level simulations. 24 The study by Mann et al. 25 used a convergence of models to address the necessary ion channel balance to match observed QTc data from long QT syndrome. Optimizing ion channel balances in our study might change the classification performance of the ML model. Changes in model prediction results during training, validation, and testing could reflect the influence of altered biomarker values on TdP risk prediction. The grid search (GS) method applied for fine‐tuning the model's hyperparameters can help achieve optimum performance despite changed biomarker inputs. Additionally, given the excellent prediction performance shown by our stacking ensemble ML model, it can also be trained, validated, and tested with in silico biomarkers derived from simulations of cardiomyocytes models for further studies. Incorporating the human‐induced pluripotent stem cell (hiPSC) model into the stacking ensemble ML to predict TdP risk or QT prolongation in humans can be an interesting direction. 26 , 27 , 28 This aligns with the CiPA‐based cardiac safety paradigm, potentially minimizing the necessity for experimental human stem cell evaluation. Using our approach, the prediction performance of hiPSC‐based evaluation can be optimized for valuable outcomes.
